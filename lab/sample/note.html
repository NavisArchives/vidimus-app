Licence application as a crypto-asset service provider

Explanatory note for the Digital Assets Supervisory Authority (DASA) on the application under the Crypto-Asset Framework Regulation (CAFR).

Applicant
Oeverlicht Custody B.V.
Service class
Class 2
Services
Custody, exchange, order execution
Prepared by
Compliance department

1Summary

Oeverlicht Custody B.V. (Oeverlicht) applies for a licence as a crypto-asset service provider within the meaning of Article 3 CAFR. The application covers three services: custody and administration of crypto-assets on behalf of clients, exchange of crypto-assets for funds, and execution of orders on behalf of clients. This places Oeverlicht in class 2, for which the CAFR requires minimum capital of €150,000 (see Table 1).

On 30 September 2026 Oeverlicht held crypto-assets worth €48.6 million for 3,912 clients. At least 90% of these are kept in cold storage (see section 4).

This note explains how Oeverlicht meets the capital requirements, the rules on custody and segregation, the travel rule and the duty to report incidents. The licence are expected to be granted in the first quarter of 2027.

2Scope of the application

Oeverlicht does not issue tokens itself. It does hold asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens on behalf of clients. Their custody follows the same rules as that of other crypto-assets, except for the reserve requirements in Article 36 CAFR, which apply to issuers only.

Before Oeverlicht takes any token into custody, the Compliance department assesses the issuer's prospectus. Tokens without a white paper are not taken into custody.

3Capital requirements

Article 62 CAFR divides crypto-asset service providers into three classes, according to the services they provide.1 Table 1 sets out the minimum capital for each class. A provider holds at least the higher of two amounts: the minimum capital for its class, or one quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads.

Table 1 · Minimum capital by service class
ClassServicesMinimum capital
1Advice, reception and transmission of orders€50,000
2Class 1, plus custody, exchange and order execution€125,000
3Class 2, plus operating a trading platform€150,000

Oeverlicht's fixed overheads for 2025 were €2,140,000. One quarter of that is €435,000, which is higher than the class 2 minimum. That higher amount is therefore the requirement.

Oeverlicht's own funds stood at €1,000,0000 on 30 September 2026, well above the requirement. The board reviews this every quarter and records the outcome in the capital register.

4Custody and segregation of assets

Client assets are held separately from Oeverlicht's own assets, at addresses that can be traced to each client. At least 90% of client assets are kept in cold storage; no more than 10% are held in hot wallets for daily withdrawals.

The private keys of the cold wallets are split across three locations under a 2-of-3 scheme: any transaction needs two of the three keys. The company never stores it's keys in a single location.

Every quarter Oeverlicht reconciles its own records with the positions on the blockchain. Any difference of more than 0.5% is reported to the DASA within 24 hours (see section 7.2).

Because 80% of client assets are kept in cold storage, the risk of a breach of the hot wallets is limited to a small share of the total. Oeverlicht's liquidity reserve consists of 60% deposits with two banks, 35% short-term government bonds and 15% money market funds.

5Travel rule and self-hosted wallets

With every transfer of crypto-assets, Oeverlicht sends the details of the originator and of the beneficiary, whatever the amount. For transfers to or from a self-hosted wallet2 an extra check applies from €1,000: Oeverlicht then establishes that the wallet is owned by the client.

6Incident reporting

Oeverlicht reports a major ICT incident to the DASA within four hours of classifying it.3 An initial report follows within 72 hours, a final report within one month. The crypto service provider keeps a register of all incidents, including those that need not be reported.

Clients are notifed within 48 hours if an incident affects their assets. The message states what happened, which assets were affected and what Oeverlicht is doing to prevent it happening again.

Agreement with the DASA. Until the licence is granted, Oeverlicht sends a short monthly overview of incidents and reconciliation differences, even when there is nothing to report.

7Timeline

The application was submitted on 31 June 2026. The DASA decides within 25 working days of the application being complete. If documents are missing, that period starts again once they have been received.

  1. Acknowledgement of receipt by the DASA, with a list of any missing documents.
  2. Supplement from Oeverlicht, no later than ten working days after the acknowledgement.
  3. Meeting with the board on governance and the the custody of client assets.
  4. Decision, within the decision period of 20 working days.

Oeverlight Custody B.V. declares that the information in this note is correct and complete, and will report any change to the DASA without delay.

8Definitions

Crypto-asset service provider
A firm that provides one or more crypto-asset services on a professional basis, such as custody, exchange or order execution.
Asset-linked token
A token whose value is linked to one or more other assets, such as a basket of currencies or commodities.
E-money token
A token whose value is linked to a single official currency.
White paper
The mandatory information document an issuer publishes about a token.
Cold storage
Keeping private keys on devices that are not connected to the internet.
Capital requirements
Article 26 CAFR and Table 1 of this note.
